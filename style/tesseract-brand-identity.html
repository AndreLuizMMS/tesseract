Founding rule

Green is not a state color. Green is keyboard ownership. Neon is scarce on purpose: a single phosphor glow across the entire screen, one cell at a time. Darkness is the material — the glow only exists because almost everything else is off.

// 01the neon shift──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Two colors now, not one. The symbol is two squares — the second dimension earned its own color.

Phosphor green #55FFA6

Ownership. The front square, the cursor, the cell that holds your keyboard. One per screen.

Flux cyan #22E0D0

Structure. The back square, the grid, the section numbers, the ⧉ glyph. Never a state color — same as green.

What was left out, and why

Glow and scanlineBrand surface only — site, README, banner. A terminal doesn't emit light; inside the app the effect is fake and hurts readability
Chromatic aberrationWordmark only. Applied to UI text, it breaks the dense legibility the product promises
Digital rainOut. It's the one cyberpunk reference that became a joke — and it would steal the green from the keyboard
Green as stateStill forbidden. Neon doesn't change the semantics

// 09do & don't───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Do

  1. Darkness first. Neon only exists because 95% of the screen is off.
  2. Green = ownership, cyan = structure. Never reversed.
  3. Glow and scanline only on the brand surface, never in the terminal.
  4. Distinguish state by shape and area before color.
  5. A single phosphor glow lit per screen.
  6. Test in black and white before approving.

Don't

  1. Green or cyan as a state signal.
  2. Digital rain, decorative katakana, "ACCESS GRANTED".
  3. Glow on UI text — it kills legible density.
  4. More than one inverted badge visible at once.
  5. Neon pink. Two colors are already enough; a third turns it into arcade.
  6. The word "tesseract" alone next to OCR or imagery.
